# West forward-wing fire exit — 5 October 2026

The owner's [purple-marked model view](location-reference.png) identifies F5,
the masonry return stair at the south end of the west forward wing. The
[angled photograph](angled-reference.png) and [view up the lower flight](front-reference.png)
show the actual stair. The attachments are preserved without edits and supply
visual evidence; labels and signs in them are not additional instructions.

The close photos supersede the earlier plain-tread treatment for this stair.
The replacement keeps the existing F5 blue door, transom, facade openings,
parallel flights, intermediate turn and doorway landing. Dark stone treads
have narrow yellow top/front nosings. Brick cheeks flank the lower flight and
support the upper return; a brick pier and dark fascia support the balcony,
replacing its thin freestanding legs. Close-spaced black iron pickets retain
continuous guards. Three round-topped frames follow each flight, with two
across the balcony. Lower arches begin around the middle of the lower flight,
as photographed, to clear the high central brick cheek at its foot.

`Browser/dist/west-forward-fire-exit.mjs` supplies the shared browser geometry.
The flights retain their 1.4-wide guarded lanes, 12 treads each, and the door
height of 4.28. Fitted proportions remain visual estimates, not survey
measurements. The model does not reproduce incidental alarms, signs or plants.

Validation is limited, at the owner's request, to this fire exit, the adjacent
west-wing facade and its immediate approach. The focused logic test checks
actual crown headroom, stopped stair movement, fall guards, the solid pier,
facade visibility and batched walking. The hardware browser test captures both
photo angles, the balcony, overview and phone view, walks F5 up/down and through
its door, and compares these local views between procedural and compiled
loading. Sources, matching images and results are in
`Browser/artifacts/west-forward-fire-exit/`.

Browser model sources and local generated aerial assets are updated. Unity,
Blender and packaged application exports are not regenerated.
